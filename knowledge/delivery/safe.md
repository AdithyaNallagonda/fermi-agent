# SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) 6.x / AI-Native SAFe
Last reviewed: 2026-10 ⚠️ verify at framework.scaledagile.com and support.scaledagile.com

## 2026 status
- The framework is still SAFe 6.x at its core, but Scaled Agile **stopped using version numbers for courses**:
  courseware is identified by release date (e.g., SAFe Agilist courseware 2026.02.24).
- **AI-Native SAFe** (July 2026) adds governance and practices for the AI era; certifications are being
  re-branded **"AI-Empowered"** (e.g., Certified AI-Empowered SAFe Practitioner / Agilist). Existing SAFe 6
  certified practitioners can upgrade via a short self-paced "Essential AI Skills" SAFe Skill (~45 min) ⚠️ verify
  for each role-based cert.
- Blogs describing "SAFe 7.0" are speculation, not official releases.

## Configurations
Essential (team + ART — the core) → Large Solution (multiple ARTs + suppliers building one solution) →
Portfolio (strategy, Lean Portfolio Management, funding value streams) → Full.

## Core values (SAFe 6)
Alignment · Respect for people · Relentless improvement · Transparency. (Built-in quality is a core
competency practice; in SAFe 5 it was a core value.)

## Ten SAFe Lean-Agile Principles
1. Take an economic view 2. Apply systems thinking 3. Assume variability; preserve options 4. Build
incrementally with fast, integrated learning cycles 5. Base milestones on objective evaluation of working
systems 6. Make value flow without interruptions 7. Apply cadence, synchronize with cross-domain planning
8. Unlock the intrinsic motivation of knowledge workers 9. Decentralize decision-making 10. Organize
around value.

## Seven core competencies
Lean-Agile Leadership · Team and Technical Agility · Agile Product Delivery · Enterprise Solution Delivery ·
Lean Portfolio Management · Organizational Agility · Continuous Learning Culture.

## Key constructs
- **Agile Release Train (ART)**: 50–125 people, 5–12 agile teams, aligned to a value stream.
- **Planning Interval (PI)** (renamed from Program Increment in SAFe 6): typically 8–12 weeks (commonly
  4 development iterations + 1 Innovation & Planning (IP) iteration).
- **PI Planning**: 2-day event; business context, product vision, architecture vision; team breakouts;
  draft plans; management review & problem solving; final plans; **ROAM** risks; **confidence vote**
  (fist of five); program board shows features & dependencies.
- **Inspect & Adapt**: PI system demo, quantitative/qualitative measurement, problem-solving workshop.
- Roles: Release Train Engineer (RTE — chief Scrum Master of the ART), Product Management, System Architect,
  Business Owners, Product Owner, Scrum Master/Team Coach, Solution Train Engineer, Epic Owners,
  Enterprise Architect, LPM.
- Backlog hierarchy: Epics (portfolio, Lean business case, MVP, hypothesis) → Capabilities (large
  solution) → Features (ART, benefit hypothesis) → Stories (team). Enablers (exploration, architecture,
  infrastructure, compliance).
- **Architectural Runway**: enablers that build technical foundation ahead of features.
- **PI Objectives**: committed & uncommitted (stretch); business value assigned by Business Owners;
  predictability measure = actual BV / planned BV (target 80–100%).
- **WSJF** = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size; CoD = User-Business Value + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction &/or
  Opportunity Enablement (relative Fibonacci estimates).
- **Flow metrics** (SAFe 6): Flow Distribution, Flow Velocity, Flow Time, Flow Load, Flow Efficiency, Flow
  Predictability.
- **Continuous Delivery Pipeline**: Continuous Exploration → Continuous Integration → Continuous
  Deployment → Release on Demand (with DevOps / CALMR: Culture, Automation, Lean flow, Measurement,
  Recovery).
- Lean Portfolio Management: strategy & investment funding (participatory budgeting, guardrails), Agile
  portfolio operations, Lean governance; Portfolio Kanban; OKRs integrated in SAFe 6.

## Practical coaching tips
- Don't launch an ART without trained leadership and identified value streams (Value Stream & ART
  Identification workshop).
- Keep IP iteration sacred (not a buffer for spillover).
- Dependencies on the program board are the main risk signal — reduce them via team topology.
- Normalize story points only for capacity planning, never to compare teams.
